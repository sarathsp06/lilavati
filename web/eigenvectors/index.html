What eigenvectors and eigenvalues are

An eigenvector of a matrix is an arrow whose direction the matrix's move leaves unchanged, and its eigenvalue is the number the arrow gets multiplied by.

  • Most arrows get turned: after the move, the arrow A v (the move A applied to the arrow v) points somewhere else than v did.
  • An eigenvector stays on its own line through the origin: A v = λ·v, read "A applied to v equals λ times v". λ (lambda) is the eigenvalue.
  • The eigenvalue says what happens along that line: 3 means three times longer, ½ half as long, −1 flipped round, 0 squashed to the origin.
  • How many: a 2×2 matrix has at most two such lines (unless it stretches every direction equally), and a pure turn has none.

Finding the directions a matrix doesn't turn, step by step

These pictures follow what you set up above: change it and they change.

1Most arrows get turned

Take arrows pointing every way round a circle and apply the matrix. Most land pointing in a new direction. That is what makes a matrix hard to read at a glance.

2A few stay on their own line

Some directions come out pointing the same way (or exactly backwards): Av = λv. These are the eigenvectors, from the German eigen, "own": the matrix's own directions.

Any arrow along that line works, so really an eigenvector is a whole line. A 2×2 matrix has at most two such lines.

3The eigenvalue is the stretch

λ = 2 doubles the arrow, λ = 0.5 halves it, λ = −1 flips it to point backwards, λ = 0 squashes it to nothing. A turn has no real eigenvalues at all, because it turns every direction.

4Finding them: where the determinant hits zero

Av = λv means (A − λI)v = 0: the matrix A − λI squashes v to nothing. A matrix squashes something only if its determinant is 0. For a 2×2 matrix that gives a quadratic: λ² − (a + d)λ + (ad − bc) = 0.

Its two roots add up to a + d (the trace) and multiply to ad − bc (the determinant).

5Apply it again and again

Apply a matrix many times and the part along the biggest eigenvector grows fastest, so any starting arrow swings round to it. This is power iteration, and it is how PageRank found the most important web pages.

Rabbits too: the matrix [[1, 1], [1, 0]] makes Fibonacci numbers, 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, …, and its biggest eigenvalue, the golden ratio 1.618…, is how fast they grow.

6In its own directions, a matrix just stretches

Use the eigenvectors as your grid. Then the matrix only stretches along each grid direction: it becomes a diagonal matrix. This is the eigendecomposition A = P D P⁻¹, and it makes powers easy: Aⁿ = P Dⁿ P⁻¹.

Symmetric matrices are the best case: their eigenvectors are always at right angles. Covariance matrices in data science are symmetric, which is why PCA works.

In symbols

Reading the symbols: A is the matrix, v an arrow, λ (lambda) a plain number, so A v = λ v reads "A applied to v gives λ times v". λ₁, λ₂ are the two eigenvalues; λ₁·λ₂ is their product. I is the do-nothing matrix. Aⁿ is A applied n times.

A v = λ v

In words: v is only stretched by λ, not turned.

det(A − λI) = 0

In words: the equation the eigenvalues solve.

λ₁ + λ₂ = a + d, λ₁·λ₂ = a·d − b·c

In words: they add up to the trace and multiply to the determinant.

A = P D P⁻¹

In words: eigendecomposition: change to eigen-directions, stretch, change back.

Aⁿ = P Dⁿ P⁻¹

In words: repeating the move just raises each stretch to a power.

A = Q Λ Qᵀ (A symmetric)

In words: for symmetric matrices the directions are at right angles, so Q is a turn.

Where you meet it

  • Google search. PageRank scored every web page by the biggest eigenvector of a huge matrix of links between pages.
  • Data science. Principal component analysis finds the eigenvectors of the data's covariance matrix: the directions it varies most.
  • Training neural networks. When a signal is multiplied by the same matrix again and again, eigenvalues above 1 make it explode and below 1 make it vanish: the classic problem of recurrent networks.
  • Bridges and guitars. The ways a structure or a string likes to vibrate are eigenvectors; the eigenvalues give the frequencies.
  • Quantum physics. The energy levels of an atom are the eigenvalues of its energy matrix.

Fun facts

"Eigen" means own

David Hilbert used the German word eigen in this sense in 1904. English kept half of it: eigenvalue, eigenvector.

The $25,000,000,000 eigenvector

That is the title of a 2006 paper by Kurt Bryan and Tanya Leise explaining that Google's PageRank is an eigenvector problem.

Eigenfaces, 1991

Matthew Turk and Alex Pentland recognised faces by describing each one as a mix of a few eigenvectors of many face photos, "eigenfaces".

The golden ratio in rabbits

Fibonacci's rabbit numbers grow by the golden ratio 1.618… each step, because that is the biggest eigenvalue of the matrix [[1, 1], [1, 0]].

Common mix-ups

  • Not: "Every matrix has eigenvectors you can draw." But: a turn moves every direction, so it has none in the real plane (its eigenvalues are complex numbers).
  • Not: "An eigenvector is one particular arrow." But: any multiple of it works too; it is really a whole line.
  • Not: "An eigenvalue of 0 isn't allowed." But: it is: it means the matrix squashes that direction flat. Only the zero vector is never called an eigenvector.
